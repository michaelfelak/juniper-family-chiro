Webster Analysis

Pelvic imbalance during pregnancy can lead to altered nerve function to the uterus, as well as changes to the bony and soft tissue structures of the pelvis. Altered biomechanics can affect the baby’s ability to get into the optimal position for birth.

Undue tension on the uterus, altered pelvic biomechanics, and baby positioning are the three main causes of dystocia, or difficult labor. The Webster Analysis restores proper biomechanics to the pelvis and its supporting structures. This allows for a safer, easier birth.

The Webster Analysis can be used throughout pregnancy, but the earlier it is implemented, the more likely you are to benefit from care.

Birth preparation

Power, Passage & Passenger

Three important factors work together during labor and birth: Power, Passage, and Passenger. Understanding each one helps explain why pelvic balance, nervous system function, and baby’s position are an important part of preparing your body for birth.

Power

Your nervous system coordinates the muscles involved in labor and birth. Chiropractic care supports optimal nervous system function as your body prepares for delivery.

Passage

Your pelvis is the passage your baby moves through during birth. Reducing tension in the pelvis and surrounding soft tissue helps restore balance and proper movement, allowing your pelvis to function as it was designed.

Passenger

Your baby’s position can play an important role in labor and birth. By restoring balance to the pelvis and surrounding structures, Webster care helps create the best possible environment for baby to find an optimal position for birth.

Pregnancy FAQ

What moms often ask us

Is chiropractic care safe during pregnancy?

Yes. Chiropractic care is safe during every phase of pregnancy. We use gentle techniques, pregnancy-specific positioning, and specially designed pillows to keep you comfortable as your body changes.

Can chiropractic care help with pregnancy discomfort?

Yes. Many moms seek chiropractic care to help alleviate discomfort and support their bodies as they adapt to the physical changes of pregnancy. Care is always individualized to you and your stage of pregnancy.

Does the Webster Analysis turn breech babies?

No. The Webster Analysis does not turn or reposition babies. Instead, it focuses on restoring proper biomechanics to the pelvis and surrounding structures, creating the best possible environment for baby to find an optimal position for birth.

Research has reported resolution of breech positioning in approximately 92% of cases following the use of the Webster Analysis.

Source: Pistolese RA. The Webster Technique: a chiropractic technique with obstetric implications. J Manipulative Physiol Ther. 2002;25(6):E1-E9.

How long will I need chiropractic care?

Every pregnancy and every mom is different. We’ll make recommendations based on your individual needs, stage of pregnancy, and goals for care.

As your pregnancy progresses, we’ll continue to reassess and adjust your care plan as needed.

Why should I continue care after my baby is born?

Birth is a major physical event, and the postpartum season brings new physical, chemical, and emotional stressors.

Continuing care after birth can support your body as it recovers and adapts to the demands of motherhood.
